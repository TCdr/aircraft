1. Clearer failure names

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Failures  |  Status: Not sim-tested (labels only).

2. Transponder failures

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Failures > ATA 34 Navigation; pedestal ATC/TCAS panel; E/WD, STATUS  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-04).

What it is for

Two new failures, ATC/XPDR 1 and ATC/XPDR 2. MSFS has a single transponder: it stands for the transponder selected on the ATC panel (SYS 1 / SYS 2). When the selected one fails, the sim transponder stops replying and the ATC FAIL light comes on; selecting the other system brings the replies back. With both lost, the TCAS goes to standby. The ECAM alerts and STATUS follow the A320 FCOM PRO-ABN-NAV procedures.

Fix on the way: the ATC/XPDR STBY indication read a second sim transponder that does not exist; the standby state of each transponder now comes from the system selection and the mode selector.

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Failures > ATA 23 Communication; pedestal RMP 1 and RMP 2  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-04).

What it is for

Five new ATA 23 failures: RMP 1, RMP 2, VHF 1, VHF 2 and VHF 3 (same ids as the A380X ATA 23 failures). A failed RMP goes blank and stops tuning; the other RMP still tunes every VHF (A320 FCOM DSC-23-10-20: "If one RMP fails, the remaining one controls all the VHF transceivers"). A failed VHF switches its MSFS COM radio off: it neither receives nor transmits.

5. Fire detection model and fire failures

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Failures > ATA 26 Fire Protection; overhead FIRE panel; ENG MASTER panel; E/WD, STATUS  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-04).

What it is for

Nine new failures: Engine 1 fire, Engine 2 fire, APU fire, and Engine 1 fire detection loop A, Engine 1 fire detection loop B, Engine 2 fire detection loop A, Engine 2 fire detection loop B, APU fire detection loop A, APU fire detection loop B.

Each zone now has the two detection loops and the fire detection unit of the aircraft (A320 FCOM DSC-26-20-10). A fire warning comes when both loops detect the fire, when one loop detects it and the other one is failed, or when both loops break within 5 s (flame effect). The logic moved from the A380X into the common code; the A380X behaviour is unchanged. The fires of the MSFS failures menu still give the warnings, through the loops. The FIRE TEST is unchanged.

  • The ENG MASTER panel FIRE light comes on with the engine fire warning (before: FIRE TEST only).
  • APU fire on the ground: the APU shuts down and the APU AGENT discharges automatically 3 s after the warning (FCOM DSC-26-20-20).
  • Each AGENT discharge has one chance in two to put a flyPad fire out.
  • Loop supplies (FCOM PRO-ABN-ELEC INOP SYS): DC BUS 2 for ENG 1 LOOP B and ENG 2 LOOP A, DC ESS BUS for ENG 1 LOOP A and ENG 2 LOOP B, DC BAT BUS for both APU loops. An unpowered loop is a failed loop.

What you should see

SituationWhat you should see
Engine 1 fireENG 1 FIRE warning (from core/fire), red ENG 1 FIRE pushbutton light and red FIRE light on the ENG MASTER panel.
APU fire on the groundAPU FIRE warning; the APU shuts down; 3 s later the APU AGENT discharges (DISCH light).
AGENT dischargedThe fire goes out, or not (one chance in two per discharge).
One loop failedAmber ENG 1 FIRE LOOP A FAULT (LOOP B, ENG 2, APU FIRE LOOP A / B); STATUS INOP SYS ENG 1 LOOP A (ENG 1 LOOP B, ENG 2 LOOP A / B, APU LOOP A / B). A fire is still detected by the other loop.
Both loops of a zone failedENG 1 FIRE DET FAULT (ENG 2, APU FIRE DET FAULT) instead of the two loop cautions; STATUS INOP SYS FIRE DET 1 (FIRE DET 2, APU FIRE DET).

  1. Open flyPad > Failures > ATA 26 Fire Protection and activate a fire or a loop failure.
  2. Fight a fire with the ECAM procedure (FIRE pushbutton, AGENT 1, AGENT 2). Deactivating the failure also ends the fire.

6. Fire detection unit failures, FIRE TEST light logic, APU fire horn

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Failures > ATA 26 Fire Protection; overhead FIRE panel; ENG MASTER panel; E/WD, STATUS  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-04); the horn sound itself is silent in MSFS 2024. Rust tests (fire_protection), unit tests: FireDetectionFaults.spec.ts, EngineMasterFireLight.spec.ts.

What it is for

Three follow-ups of the fire detection model above:

  • Fire detection unit failures: three new failures, Engine 1 fire detection unit (FDU), Engine 2 fire detection unit (FDU) and APU fire detection unit (FDU). A failed FDU gives the FIRE DET FAULT caution of its zone even with healthy loops, and detects no fire. Clearing the failure with a fire present gives the fire warning at once.
  • ENG MASTER panel FIRE light during the FIRE TEST: the A320 FCOM (DSC-26-20-20, ENG 1(2) FIRE TEST pb) says that the FIRE lights do not come on with an engine 1 loop A fault or an engine 2 loop B fault. The light now stays dark in those cases (a loop fault is its failure or the loss of its DC ESS supply).
  • APU fire external horn: an APU fire on the ground sounds the horn in the nose gear bay (FCOM DSC-26-20-20 APU FIRE light, DSC-49-10-20), on the same sound as the MECH call; not during the FIRE TEST.

Five new failures: L TK pump 1, L TK pump 2, R TK pump 1, R TK pump 2 and X FEED valve jammed in place. The FUEL panel pushbuttons now set a selection, and the systems host moves the MSFS pump or valve switch from it: a failed pump is held off, the jammed valve is held where it was. The FAULT lights, the SD FUEL page and the cautions compare the selection with the real state (A320 FCOM PRO-ABN-FUEL).

What it is for

Two new failures, CTR TK L XFR valve jammed in place and CTR TK R XFR valve jammed in place: the centre tank transfer valve of that side stays open or closed, whatever its CTR TK XFR pushbutton commands. The FWC raises the A320 FCOM PRO-ABN-FUEL cautions: FUEL CTR L(R) XFR FAULT (valve not fully closed, or not fully open) and, with both valves stuck the same way, FUEL CTR L + R XFR FAULT, which replaces the two single ones. What it is for

Six new failures: Engine 1 oil leak, Engine 2 oil leak, Engine 1 oil filter clog, Engine 2 oil filter clog, Engine 1 oil overheat and Engine 2 oil overheat, with the oil indications and the ECAM alerts of the A320 FCOM (DSC-70-90-40, PRO-ABN-ENG).

The FADEC computes the oil quantity, pressure and temperature again: its oil model had been dead since a FADEC migration and is restored (from the A380X FADEC, with the A32NX engine imbalance effect on the oil pressure). The failures act on that model:

  • Leak: the oil system empties at 2 qt per minute of simulation time (the SD tank reading falls about 1.6 qt/min). The pump keeps its normal pressure down to 2.5 qt in the whole system and gives none at 0.6 qt, so the pressure falls once the tank reads nearly empty.
  • Filter clog: CLOG on the SD ENGINE page and the ENG OIL FILTER CLOG caution, no other effect.
  • Overheat: the oil goes to 135 °C at idle and 175 °C at 100 % N2 (about 165 °C at cruise), with a 60 s time constant: ENG OIL HI TEMP after about 2 minutes in cruise; THR LEVER IDLE brings it below 155 °C within about 30 s. Once the failure is cleared the oil cools down gradually to its normal temperature.
  • SD ENGINE page: the FCOM 0-22 QT scale; the quantity pulses below 3.25 QT (until 4.75 QT); the oil pressure red below 13 PSI, pulsing between 13 and 16 PSI with N2 above 75 % (until it is back above 20 PSI); amber CLOG under the oil pressure. The CRUISE page shows the same FADEC oil quantity.
  • Fixes found in the sim tests: the SD oil quantity read nearly empty in every climb (the oil gulping formula got the thrust in newtons instead of pounds), and the pressure followed the tank reading, which gave a false ENG OIL LO PR in a normal climb: the pump pressure now follows the oil of the whole system. The overheat stopped far below its target because MSFS pulls the oil temperature back to its own model: the FADEC now keeps its own overheat temperature.
  • The start N2 and fuel flow of the FADEC model also blend smoothly at low N2 now (they overshot below about 10 % N2).
