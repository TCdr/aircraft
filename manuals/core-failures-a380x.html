4. Trim air duct overheat and jammed valve alerts

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: overhead AIR panel (HOT AIR 1 / 2, PACK 1 / 2); E/WD, STATUS; SD COND page  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-04). Rust tests (air_conditioning), unit tests: CondTrimAirFlags.spec.ts, CondTrimAirMarks.spec.ts.

What it is for

The trim air failures above now give the alerts of the A380 FCOM (PRO-ABN-ECAM-10-21-10). The temperature controller monitors each trim air duct and valve: a duct above 70 deg C for 1.2 s is an overheat, latched until its HOT AIR pushbutton is set OFF; a valve that does not follow its demand is jammed. The FWS reads both from the controller and raises:

  • COND DUCT OVHT (master caution, single chime, FAULT light in the HOT AIR pushbutton, SD COND page) with the CKPT DUCT OVHT, CABIN DUCT OVHT and FWD CARGO DUCT OVHT cases, the deferred procedure WHEN DUCT OVHT OUT, and its STATUS lines (INOP SYS, CABIN TEMP REGUL DEGRADED, FWD CRG TEMP REGUL DEGRADED).
  • COND FWD CARGO TEMP REGUL FAULT for a jammed forward cargo trim air valve: CARGO TRIM AIR VLV FAULT, CARGO TEMP ... MONITOR, STATUS INFO FWD CRG TEMP REGUL DEGRADED (SD COND page only: no chime, no master light).
  • SD COND page: the amber OVHT mark of an overheated cockpit or cabin zone duct and the trim air valve mark of a jammed valve (before: never shown).

What changed after the first sim tests

  • No N3 overshoot at the end of an in-flight start. The three in-flight relights of 2026-10-05 took N3 from 61 % to 97.5 % for 5 to 7 s with the levers in CL: while the engine was out the FADEC thrust loop wound the MSFS throttle up to 100 %. Now the MSFS throttle stays at idle while the engine is out or starting and until it runs at idle, then the engine accelerates to the lever or A/THR demand (A380 FCOM DSC-70-30 "FADEC stabilizes the engine at idle speed"; ENG RELIGHT IN FLIGHT: THR LEVER IDLE, then WHEN IDLE REACHED).
  • TWO ENGS OUT STATUS lists the FCOM lines PART SPLRs, PART L/G RETRACTION, G (Y) HYD SYS, PACK 1 (2) and one GEN line for the two generators lost (GEN 1+2, 3+4 on the same side; GEN 1+3, 1+4, 2+3, 2+4 on opposite sides).
  • ALL ENG FLAME OUT: its RAT MAN ON line is sensed (it turns green when the RAT is deployed), it has the missing L/G GRVTY (EXTN 2 MIN) DOWN line before a forced landing, and it inhibits ELEC EMER CONFIG as in the FCOM. The ELEC EMER CONFIG alert itself is on core/a380x-elec: this branch only computes the inhibited signal, so the inhibition shows once both branches are merged.
  • ENG RELIGHT IN FLIGHT shows the single engine limits MAX GUARANTEED ALTITUDE : 30000 FT and MIN SPEED FOR WINDML RELIGHT : 260 KT (FCOM l.174902-174904).
  • No starter air without a source. A starter assisted relight was granted with no APU or crossbleed air: the air trapped in a stopped engine bleed duct and the slowly venting starter duct (about 45 s) kept the starter pressurised. The duct of an engine with no air source now empties within about 3 s, and the starter duct is empty (below 5 PSIG) about 3 s after the start valve closes (design values: the FCOM gives none).
  • X BLEED selector OPEN opens the crossbleed valves. Their manual control used the A320 DC ESS SHED busbar, which the A380 does not have; it now uses DC ESS (A380 FCOM DSC-36-30). The crossbleed starter air of a starter assisted relight depends on it.
  • The FADECs read the ENG START selector. They read a non-existent variable (always 0) and never saw the selector at IGN START; a static check now guards the variable names.
  • Each FADEC reads the LGCIU of its side: engines 1 and 2 LGCIU 1, engines 3 and 4 LGCIU 2 (design choice, the FCOM does not say). Before, the on-ground input of engines 3 and 4 was read past the end of the two-LGCIU list.
  • ENG MASTER OFF closes the HP fuel valve and the engine stops burning at once: see "HP fuel valve" below.

Design choices

  • The envelope governs every in-flight relight, also after a crew shutdown without any failure: MSFS would otherwise relight anywhere.
  • The quick relight also works in zone 3, where the windmill alone lights the engine.
  • A windmilling engine keeps N3 = 11 % x IAS / 260 kt and N1 = 1.5 x N3 (the FCOM gives no values).
  • FBW models the Trent 972 (three spools): the FCOM N2 (GP7200 core) is read on the Trent N3. ENG FAIL keeps the N3 < 50 % threshold of FBW (the FCOM says N2 below 61 %).
  • TWO ENGS OUT is shown only with exactly two engines out (with three, ENG FAIL RELIGHT PROC APPLY; with four, ALL ENG FLAME OUT).
  • During a windmilling start the two engine pumps are off-loaded (depressurised), not disconnected: the ENG PUMPS DISC clutch cannot be reconnected in flight.
  • Once a relight is granted in flight the FADEC holds the MSFS core at 25 % N2: MSFS only burns above 20 % N2, and its starter only turns with the APU bleed, so without it a windmilling or crossbleed relight would never burn.
  • The throttle hold of an in-flight start (idle until the engine runs) works in flight only; a fallback ends it for a start that settles just below the FBW idle N3.
  • With the ENG MASTER OFF in flight the MSFS core runs down to the windmill speed: also so on develop, not a fault.

8. Automatic relight with both igniters, and the engine feed tank fuel draw

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Automatic (FADEC); pedestal ENG MASTER levers; E/WD  |  Status: Auto relight sim-tested OK on develop 2026-10-06 (relight batch T3, recorded: ignition 0.5 s after the combustion loss with the ENG START selector at NORM, relit when the fuel came back, ignition off 60 s later). Native tests auto_relight_test (recorded flameout replay), feed_tank_draw_test. The core branch itself was not sim-tested.

What it is for

In a flight of 2026-10-06 MSFS dropped the combustion of all four engines by itself at 7 900 ft. With the ENG START selector at NORM nothing relit them: the engines windmilled for 150 s until the crew selected IGN START. Two changes:

  • AUTO RELIGHT (A380 FCOM DSC-70-80-30-20 QUICK AND AUTO RELIGHT: "If the FADEC detects an engine flame out, on ground or in flight, continuous ignition with both igniters is automatically selected. The ignition is maintained for 60 s after engine relight."). When a running engine (ENG MASTER ON, FADEC supplied, fuel not cut) loses its combustion for 0.5 s, its FADEC turns on both igniters whatever the ENG START selector position, until 60 s of continuous combustion after the relight.
  • Feed tank draw. Each engine is fed by MSFS through a 1 gallon buffer tank that the FADEC drains by the fuel burned. The FADEC took the whole burn of an update at once and compared kilograms with pounds, so one long sim frame could empty the four buffer tanks together and leave no fuel at any engine (the suspected cause of the four-engine flameout). Now, while the feed tank holds fuel, the FADEC leaves a small quantity in the buffer and carries the rest of the burn over to the next updates, so the fuel burned is still taken in full. A dry feed tank still empties the buffer and the engine flames out (fuel exhaustion).

11. HP fuel valve: no engine burning without a FADEC start

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Pedestal ENG MASTER levers and ENG START selector  |  Status: Sim-tested on develop: the fault found 2026-10-06 (Flight A S2 T3, a restart 34 s after a shutdown skipped the start sequence); 2026-10-07 restart after shutdown OK (full start sequence, igniter B), ENG MASTER ON at NORM stays dark, a start at IGN START opens the HP valve at the fuel-on point; 2026-10-07 evening the HP valves stayed closed from load through power-up (more than 200 s). Rust tests (engine_failure.rs HpFuelValve, sim_fuel_valve.rs), native start_sequence tests. The core branch itself was not sim-tested.

What it is for

A380 FCOM DSC-70-30 ENGINE SHUTDOWN: with the ENG MASTER OFF "The FADEC closes the LP and HP fuel valves. The engine decelerates and stops." The ENG MASTER only closed an MSFS valve upstream of the engine buffer tank, so MSFS kept burning a shut down engine for 20 to 60 s, and a restart in that time skipped the start sequence. And with the ENG START selector at NORM, ENG MASTER ON on the ground let the MSFS starter turn the core and MSFS lit it at 22 % N2 without any FADEC start (the FCOM: IGN START first, fuel at 20 %; at NORM the MASTER ON starts nothing).

  • The HP fuel valve of each engine (the MSFS fuel valves 60-63 after the buffer tanks) is now closed with the ENG MASTER OFF or the fuel cut, and on the ground until the FADEC start sequence reaches its fuel-on point (IGN START then MASTER ON) or the quick relight opens it (MASTER OFF then ON within 30 s above 45 % N3). It is open for the aircraft presets and a flight loaded with the master ON. In flight the relight logic keeps the fuel cut until the engine may light up. Once open it stays open until the MASTER OFF or a fuel cut, so a running engine keeps its fuel at NORM and its auto relight has fuel.
  • MSFS could reopen a closed valve by itself a few seconds later (flight load, power-up of the valve circuit). The systems now watch the MSFS valve switch and command it again whenever it disagrees, and once on the first update of a flight.
  • An engine is shown running (ON) only when MSFS really burns in it: a core turned past 20 % by the MSFS starter at NORM no longer shows ON unlit (and no longer triggers the auto relight).

ENG MASTER ON on the ground with the ENG START selector at NORMNothing lights up: no fuel flow, no EGT.
