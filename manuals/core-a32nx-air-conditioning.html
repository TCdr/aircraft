1. Air conditioning controller per the FCOM

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: Overhead AIR COND panel, ENG FIRE pb; SD BLEED / COND  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-02): trim air valves, galley fan, FIRE pb per side, 30 s reopening. Fourth excursion and valve disagree: unit tests only.

What it is for

An audit of the air conditioning system controllers (ACSC) against A320 FCOM DSC-21-10 and PRO-ABN-AIR / PRO-ABN-COND found six deviations. All six are fixed:

  • ENG FIRE pushbutton: each one closes the pack of its own side (DSC-21-10-50). Before, the pack flow controller read the ENG 1 FIRE pb for both packs: ENG 2 FIRE closed pack 1.
  • Pack valves after an engine start: on the ground they reopen 30 s after the engine start (DSC-21-10-50 PACK pb: "reopening of the valves is delayed for 30 s to avoid a supplementary pack closure cycle during second engine start"); in flight at once.
  • Hot air valve and trim air valve failures: the hot air pressure regulating valve closes when the cockpit trim air valve fails or both cabin ones fail, and stays open with one cabin trim air valve failed (DSC-21-10-20). Before, it closed on any trim air valve failure.
  • LAV + GALLEY FAN fault: the cabin temperature sensors are no longer ventilated, so the FWD / AFT CABIN selectors set the cabin duct temperature directly (it was fixed at 15 °C). With ACSC 2 also lost, the cabin gets the cockpit duct air (PRO-ABN-COND).
  • DUCT OVHT also triggers on the fourth excursion of a duct temperature above 80 °C in one flight, not only above 88 °C (PRO-ABN-COND). The count restarts at each takeoff run.
  • PACK 1(2) FAULT also triggers when the pack flow control valve position disagrees with its command, not only on the loss of both lanes of the ACSC (PRO-ABN-AIR).

How to use

  1. Nothing new to set: use the AIR COND panel as usual (PACK 1 / 2, HOT AIR, COCKPIT / FWD CABIN / AFT CABIN).
  2. In an engine fire the ENG FIRE pb closes the pack of that side only.

2. COND page trim air valve XX and air conditioning STATUS lines

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: SD COND page; E/WD; SD STATUS page  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-02).

What it is for

FCOM DSC-21-10-50 (COND page, zone trim air valve): "The arrow is green. It is replaced by amber crosses ("XX") if the valve fails". The FBW page had a TODO there. It now shows amber XX for a valve that either ACSC reports failed.

The air conditioning alerts now fill the ECAM STATUS page (PRO-ABN-AIR, PRO-ABN-COND). The STATUS page gains a limitations part (blue, before the information):

  • COND HOT AIR FAULT gets the full FCOM procedure: -HOT AIR...OFF, then ".IF HOT AIR STILL OPEN AND DUCT OVHT PERSISTS:" -PACK 1 OFF, -PACK 2 OFF, -DES TO FL 100/MEA-MORA, ".WHEN DIFF PR <1 PSI AND FL BELOW 100:" -RAM AIR ON, MAX FL 100/MEA-MORA (the descent lines in flight only).
  • The TRIM AIR SYS FAULT line "CPKT TRIM VALVE" is now spelled CKPT TRIM VALVE.
  • The FCOM FWD CRG HEAT line is left out: the FBW A320 has no cargo heating.

How to use

  1. After an air conditioning alert, clear it with CLR and read the STATUS page (it comes up by itself, or press STS).
  2. On the SD COND page, an amber XX in place of a zone arrow means that trim air valve has failed.

3. Packs regulate cockpit and cabin separately with the hot air valve closed

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: Overhead HOT AIR pb and temperature selectors; SD BLEED  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-02): HOT AIR OFF pack 1 16 °C / pack 2 29 °C; ON both back to the same value within 60 s.

What it is for

With the hot air pressure regulating valve closed (failure, HOT AIR pb OFF or the automatic closure), the trim air valves cannot mix the zones. FCOM DSC-21-10-40 (hot air valve failed closed): pack 1 regulates to the cockpit demand and pack 2 to the mean of the FWD and AFT cabin demands. With ACSC 2 lost, pack 2 follows the cockpit demand (PRO-ABN-COND). With the valve open, both packs still supply the lowest zone demand (DSC-21-10-20). Before, both packs always followed the lowest demand.

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
The zone temperatures do not separate as much as the pack outlets.The FBW air mixer gives every zone the same air, so pack 1 cannot cool the cockpit alone.Read the effect on the BLEED page pack outlet temperatures.

How to use

  1. With the HOT AIR pb OFF (or the valve failed closed), set the COCKPIT selector for the cockpit and the FWD / AFT CABIN selectors for the cabin: pack 1 serves the cockpit, pack 2 the cabin.

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1A32NX, packs running, SD BLEED page. HOT AIR pb ON. Set COCKPIT cold, FWD and AFT CABIN hot.Both pack outlet temperatures are the same (the lowest demand).
2Set the HOT AIR pb OFF and wait about a minute.Pack 1 outlet colder (cockpit demand), pack 2 warmer (cabin demand): for example 16 °C and 29 °C.
3Set the HOT AIR pb ON again.Both pack outlets go back to the same value within about 60 s.

4. BLEED page: each pack's own outlet temperature

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: SD BLEED page  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-02): HOT AIR ON 10 / 10 °C, OFF 15 / 30 °C.

What it is for

The pack outlet temperatures of the SD BLEED page were a placeholder showing the FWD cabin zone temperature on both packs. They now show the outlet temperature the air conditioning system computes for each pack, in 5 °C steps, amber above 90 °C (FCOM DSC-21-10-50, ECAM BLEED page, item 1).

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
The pack bypass valve and the compressor outlet temperature are still placeholders.The packs are not modelled internally yet.None.

How to use

  1. Open the SD BLEED page: the temperature above each pack is that pack's outlet temperature.

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1A32NX, packs running, SD BLEED page, HOT AIR pb ON.Both pack outlet values equal (for example 10), in steps of 5.
2HOT AIR pb OFF, COCKPIT cold and cabin selectors hot.The two values differ (for example 15 and 30): they are each pack's own outlet, no longer the FWD cabin temperature.
