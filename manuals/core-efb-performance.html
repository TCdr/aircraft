1. A380 takeoff calculator and company T.O data

Aircraft: A380X (the flyPad screen is shared with the A32NX, next feature)  |  Where: flyPad > Performance > Takeoff; MFD PERF T.O  |  Status: Used in the sim by the user with the company T.O data flow (2026-09-25); TOW in tonnes and the CG envelope edges tested OK (2026-10-02). Unit tests against the Airbus charts and the FCOM.

What it is for

Before each takeoff the crew needs the takeoff weight limit of the runway, the FLEX temperature (reduced thrust), the speeds V1, VR, V2 and the flaps. On the real A380 they come from the OIS takeoff application or from the airline (company takeoff data, sent to the FMS by datalink). The flyPad calculator does the computation; SEND TO FMS plays the airline: the MFD receives the data as company T.O data, and the crew checks and inserts it, as in FCOM DSC-22-FMS-20-30.

A FLEX takeoff

  1. Fill the airport and runway (or Fill data from FMS / OFP), the conditions (METAR), the TOW and the T.O CG.
  2. Keep Thrust FLEX and press Calculate.
  3. Read the FLEX temperature and the speeds in RESULTS; check the takeoff run and its margin.
  4. Send to FMS, then insert the data on the MFD (see "On the MFD: company T.O data" below).

TOGA or another FLEX temperature

The TAKEOFF RUN panel lets you look at the run for TOGA, or for any FLEX temperature between the OAT and the maximum (FCOM PER-TOF-THR-FLX). Use - / + or the slider; the speeds and the run follow.

Send to FMS sends the thrust selected for the takeoff run only (TOGA, or FLEX at its temperature), with its own speeds.

On the MFD: company T.O data

The MFD PERF T.O page gets two buttons: CPNY T.O REQUEST (ask the airline) and, once data has arrived, RECEIVED CPNY T.O.

  • After SEND T.O REQUEST the page shows REQUEST PENDING...; without an answer after 4 minutes, NO COMPANY REPLY. The flyPad Send to FMS is that answer.
  • When data arrives: the message COMPANY T.O DATA RECEIVED WAITING FOR INSERTION and the button RECEIVED CPNY T.O on PERF.

The RECEIVED COMPANY T.O DATA page

  • Up to 4 runways (arrows, RWY 1/4) × 2 thrust ratings (the TOGA / FLEX list at the top).
  • Runway in amber: not the departure runway of the active flight plan.
  • TOW in amber with UPLINK/ACTIVE TOW DISAGREE: the uplink TOW is more than 2 t below or 7 t above the FMS TOW (FUEL&LOAD).
  • TEMP shows dashes for a FLEX takeoff; T.O LIMIT is the TORA; the noise fields are filled when the procedure was sent.
  • INSERT (only when runway and TOW agree) fills PERF T.O: speeds, flaps, thrust, shift, THR RED, ACCEL, EO ACCEL and noise. It does not fill THS FOR: the crew enters the CG there. CLEAR deletes the data.
TipThe flyPad warns you right after Send to FMS if INSERT will not work: another departure runway in the FMS, no TOW in the FMS, or a TOW outside -2 t / +7 t of the FMS one.

How to use

  1. On the MFD, enter the flight plan with its departure runway and fill FUEL&LOAD (ZFW, ZFWCG, block).
  2. flyPad > Performance > Takeoff: Fill data from FMS, then METAR (or type the data).
  3. Check TOW, T.O CG, flaps and thrust; press Calculate.
  4. In TAKEOFF RUN, choose TOGA or the FLEX temperature you will use; press Send to FMS.
  5. On the MFD PERF T.O page: RECEIVED CPNY T.O, check the page, INSERT; enter the T.O CG in THS FOR.

2. A320 takeoff calculator and MCDU uplink takeoff data

Aircraft: A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Performance > Takeoff; MCDU PERF TAKE OFF > <TO DATA (6L)  |  Status: The flyPad calculation was used in the sim; the MCDU uplink pages are checked in the browser harness only; the full flow is not sim-tested. Unit tests of the takeoff distances.

What it is for

The A320 takeoff speeds, FLEX temperature and THS come from the airline or the crew's own tool. Here the flyPad computes them with the FlyByWire A320neo performance model (built from Airbus data) and sends them to the FMGC as an uplink takeoff data message, which the crew inserts from the MCDU PERF TAKE OFF page as on the real aircraft.

The flyPad screen (A320)

The screen is the one of the A380 (see "A380 takeoff calculator and company T.O data" above) with these A320 differences:

  • No noise procedure fields; T.O CG is optional: below 27 % MAC the forward CG performance applies (A320 FCOM PER-LOD) and the THS is shown in RESULTS.
  • All values come from the FBW A320 model: they are shown in green (no estimates).
  • Wet and contaminated runways are computed (the A380 page accepts dry only).
NoteAt light weights the FBW A320 model gives V1 = VR = V2 (the pictures show 148/148/148 at FLEX and 140/140/140 at TOGA for 68 t). This comes from the FlyByWire model itself (the same with the FBW version before this change), not from this feature.

Sending to the MCDU

  1. On the flyPad, choose the runway from the list (the uplink carries the runway ident), calculate, choose TOGA or the FLEX temperature for the takeoff run, and press Send to FMS.
  2. The flyPad says "Takeoff data sent to the FMS: MCDU PERF TAKE OFF, UPLINK TO DATA, RECEIVED TO DATA", and warns right away if the FMGC departure runway or TOW will prevent the insertion.
  3. On the MCDU the scratchpad shows TAKEOFF DATA UPLINK.
  4. PERF TAKE OFF > <TO DATA (6L) > RECEIVED <TO DATA (6L): the UPLINK MAX TO DATA page (or FLX TO DATA with 4R). Check it, then INSERT UPLINK.

There is no INSERT UPLINK prompt in these pictures because the harness FMGC has no flight plan. The prompt appears only when the uplink is complete (V1 and VR present) and the checks below pass.

The insertion rules (A320 FCOM)

  • The uplink runway must be the departure runway of the flight plan.
  • The uplink TOW must be not more than 1 t below or 3 t above the FMGC TOW; otherwise there is no insertion.
  • INSERT UPLINK fills V1, VR, V2, FLAPS/THS, FLEX TO TEMP (cleared for a MAX takeoff), THR RED/ACC, ENG OUT ACC and the TO SHIFT.
  • An incomplete uplink (for example without V1 or VR) gives no INSERT UPLINK prompt (DSC-22_20-70 P 6).

PERF TAKE OFF also gets the TO SHIFT field (1 m up to the runway length, CLR to delete): the distance lost when taking off from an intersection.

3. A380X company datalink reply time

Aircraft: A380X (the A32NX MCDU uplink stays instant)  |  Where: flyPad > Settings > Realism; MFD INIT, WIND, PERF T.O  |  Status: On the user's sim test list done on 2026-10-02 ("datalink"); checked in the browser harness.

What it is for

On the A380 the flight plan, the winds and the takeoff data come from the airline by datalink (FCOM DSC-22-FMS-10-40-90). A request takes time: the button shows REQUEST PENDING... until the answer, and NO COMPANY REPLY appears when nothing comes within 4 minutes. FBW answered instantly; now the answer arrives after a reply time you choose.

The setting

Real (default)60 to 120 s5 to 15 s

Airbus gives no figure: these are typical airline values, below the 4 minute limit of the FCOM.

Requesting the flight plan

  1. INIT > CPNY F-PLN REQUEST opens COMPANY F-PLN REQUEST (flight number, company route, free text).
  2. Press SEND F-PLN REQUEST. The button becomes REQUEST PENDING..., here and on INIT; one request at a time.
  3. After the reply time the SimBrief flight plan is delivered to the FMS, as before.
  4. If SimBrief does not answer (no network, no flight plan), NO COMPANY REPLY appears after 4 minutes.

Winds and takeoff data

  • CPNY WIND REQUEST (INIT or WIND page): the winds are delivered after the reply time.
  • Takeoff data (PERF T.O > CPNY T.O REQUEST, see the A380 takeoff calculator above): the answer of the flyPad Send to FMS reaches the FMS not before the reply time of the request; without a request, after the transit time only.

4. Landing calculator

Aircraft: A380X and A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Performance > Landing  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user on both aircraft: OK (2026-10-02), including the BTV exit advice on dry, wet and contaminated runways. Unit tests (mutation-tested).

What it is for

Before the descent, the crew checks that the aircraft can land on the planned runway: the landing distance for the runway condition and braking, the margin left, the approach speed VAPP and the go-around performance. On the real A380 this is the LDG PERF application of the OIS (FCOM PER-LND); on the A320 it is the QRH (in flight) and the FCOM tables (dispatch). This page does both, with the data of each aircraft.

  • IN-FLIGHT (default): the actual landing distance for the braking mode you will use, as in the QRH / OIS, with the stop margin.
  • DISPATCH: the regulatory (factored) required landing distance, to check a runway before departure.

A normal in-flight calculation (A380)

  1. In flight, with the destination and the arrival runway in the flight plan and PERF APPR filled: choose FMS and press Fill data from. Otherwise type the airport, choose the runway and fill the conditions.
  2. Check the landing weight (the FMS prediction) and the configuration.
  3. Choose the braking mode you plan to use (manual, autobrake LO / 2 / 3 / HI, or BTV).
  4. Press Calculate.

Colours follow the flyPad takeoff calculator: green values come from Airbus data; values in amber with a star (*) are estimates of a model fitted on that data (see Limitations).

Braking modes

AUTO CONF (A380, FCOM PER-LND): CONF FULL when its go-around in CONF 3 gives the required gradient, otherwise CONF 3 with a go-around in CONF 2; for an overweight landing, CONF 3 with a go-around in CONF 1+F.

Runway condition and reverse thrust

The runway condition follows the runway condition codes (RWYCC): dry, wet, water and slush 1/4" or 1/2", compacted snow, ice. The crosswind limit of the condition is shown at the top of the landing roll.

  • Reverse thrust is credited only on a contaminated runway (FCOM: no credit on dry and wet runways); the result line says REVERSE THRUST CREDIT or NO REVERSE THRUST CREDIT.
  • On the A380, BTV is prohibited on a contaminated runway (FCOM LIM-32-30): the BTV line says so and advises autobrake 3.
  • With 1/2" of water or slush, a message reminds you that Airbus does not recommend these distances: a deeper contaminant gives a shorter distance in the data.

BTV exits (A380)

On the A380, the page shows the BTV DRY and WET lines: where the aircraft stops with the BTV decelerations (FCOM DSC-32-10-30-20), from 400 m after the threshold plus 5 s of roll. When the runway comes from the list and a Navigraph account is linked, the runway exits of the airport map are drawn and rated:

ExitMeaning
RecommendedThe first exit beyond the WET line (the FCOM SOP choice, even on a dry runway).
Beyond WETAchievable wet or dry.
Dry onlyBetween the DRY and WET lines: only on a dry runway.
Not achievableBefore the DRY line.

Without the runway list or the Navigraph data, the line says "Select the runway in the list for its exits". This is information for your briefing: the BTV selection itself is made on the OANS, as on the real aircraft.

Dispatch

DISPATCH gives the required landing distance used to check a runway before departure: the actual distance divided by 0.6 on a dry runway, ×1.15 on a wet one, and on a contaminated runway the higher of the wet value and ALD ×1.15 (A380 FCOM PER-LND). Braking is manual (the braking mode is greyed out). The orange bar is the required distance.

When the runway is too short or the aircraft too heavy

The A320 version

The A320 uses the same screen with its own data: the QRH in-flight landing distances (shown ×1.15, the QRH margin) and the FCOM dispatch tables (PER-LDG-DIS-RLD) with the autoland correction and the wet minimum. Braking modes are Manual, LO and MED; the Overweight Procedure switch applies the QRH overweight landing corrections. There is no go-around section.

7. Descent calculator

Aircraft: A380X and A32NX  |  Where: flyPad > Performance > Top of Descent  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: A32NX OK (2026-10-01, after the fix of the last feature of this manual), A380X OK (2026-10-02). Unit tests.

What it is for

The FMS computes the top of descent (T/D) for the managed descent. The crew still needs other answers: how long an emergency descent takes, where to start a descent at a given V/S, whether the aircraft is late on the profile after a shortcut, and a cross-check of the FMS T/D. The A380 OIS has an in-flight descent module for this (FCOM PER-IFT-DES); this page does the same on both aircraft, and replaces the old Top of Descent calculator.

It uses the same flight and engine models as the FBW FMS predictions, so its T/D agrees with the one drawn on the ND for the same inputs.

Standard descent

  1. Press Fill data from FMS in cruise with a flight plan, or type the initial altitude, the target altitude and the weight.
  2. Choose the descent type and, if needed, the wind component and anti-ice.
  3. Press Calculate.

The deceleration to the speed limit is flown on the -1° idle path of the FMS descent strategy, which is why the profile flattens between DECEL and SPD LIM.

Descent check

Enter the distance to the target (Fill data from FMS enters the distance to the destination). The page reads the aircraft altitude and ground speed (shown top right of the box) and compares the aircraft with the profile. The aircraft is the triangle on the chart.

TipAfter a shortcut, the late message gives the V/S (or FPA) that still makes the target, to set in V/S or FPA mode, or tells you to use the speed brakes.

Given V/S, emergency and ECON

Given V/S adds a V/S field. The page flies that V/S with the thrust it needs. Where the V/S is steeper than an idle descent can give, the page flies idle there and says so in amber (the aircraft cannot descend faster at idle without speed brakes).

Emergency uses MMO / VMO with the speed brakes extended (the schedule fields turn grey).

ECON needs the FMS: it asks the FMS for its managed descent speeds. Without a flight plan in the FMS, a message says so and the page stays on the previous type.

Anti-ice, fuel factor and estimates

Anti-ice (engine or total) raises the idle thrust and flattens the descent. Its effect uses the corrections of the A320 FCOM descent table (no A380 table is public): the values it changes turn amber with a star. The fuel factor (a percentage) scales the fuel burn, like the FMS PERF FACTOR.

8. Descent calculator: T/D distance slider

Aircraft: A380X and A32NX  |  Where: Top of Descent page, under the profile  |  Status: No separate sim-test record of the slider (the descent page itself was tested OK, see above); checked in the browser harness.

What it is for

After a calculation, a quick answer to "what if I start down earlier (vectors) or later (a shortcut)?" without a new calculation: the V/S and path angle the moved descent needs, against the calculated ones.

What if the descent starts earlier or later?

After a calculation, the T/D distance slider under the profile moves the start of the descent: to the left farther from the target (earlier, e.g. vectors), to the right closer (later, e.g. a shortcut), up to 60 NM each way. The chart draws the moved top of descent dashed, straight to the target, and the line under the slider gives the V/S and the path angle it needs, to compare with the calculated ones.

  • Later: steeper than the idle descent: the figures turn amber, "speed brakes or a higher speed needed" (red when the calculation already used the speed brakes: start earlier).
  • Earlier: shallower than the idle descent: "thrust needed, more fuel".
  • The figures keep the mean ground speed of the calculated descent: an estimate for the decision, not a new calculation.

The Calculate and Clear buttons are at the bottom of the page, where they are on the takeoff and landing pages.

How to use

  1. Calculate a descent.
  2. Move the T/D distance slider under the profile: left = earlier (farther from the target), right = later.
  3. Read the line under the slider: the V/S and FPA needed, and whether speed brakes or thrust are needed.

How to test

9. Descent calculator: target waypoint from the flight plan

Aircraft: A380X and A32NX  |  Where: Top of Descent page > DESCENT > Target wpt  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-01). Unit tests.

What it is for

The target of a descent is often a waypoint with an altitude constraint (the first constraint of the STAR, a crossing restriction from ATC), not the destination. The FMS (A380 FMC, A32NX MCDU) now sends the waypoints ahead of the aircraft, up to the missed approach, with their altitude constraints and their distance along the route. The Target wpt list of the flyPad picks one as the target.

What you see

  • The list shows each waypoint with its constraint written as on the F-PLN: 9000 (at), +9000 (at or above), -9000 (at or below), 11000/9000 (window). A dash = no target waypoint (the destination).
  • Picking a waypoint sets the target altitude from its constraint (the upper altitude of a window, to the nearest 100 ft; a waypoint without a constraint keeps the target altitude you entered) and the distance to target along the flight plan.
  • The distance follows the aircraft: it is refreshed every 5 s. When the waypoint is sequenced, the target waypoint is cleared.
  • Typing a distance yourself clears the target waypoint (the distance is no longer the waypoint's).

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
The waypoints need the FMS.They come from the active flight plan.Without a flight plan the list holds only the dash.
Climb constraints of the SID are not used as targets.An "at or above altitude 2" constraint is a climb constraint.-

How to use

  1. In cruise, with a STAR in the flight plan: Top of Descent page, Fill data from FMS.
  2. Open Target wpt and pick the waypoint with the constraint you must meet.
  3. Press Calculate and watch the DESCENT CHECK line as you fly.
