1. A/THR disconnect, EVAC HORN, DFDR EVENT and ACMS TRIGGER pushbuttons work

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Thrust levers 1 and 4, overhead EVAC panel, right pedestal  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-03).

What it is for

The cockpit behaviour XML named model nodes or animations that are not in the A380X cockpit model, so these pushbuttons could not be clicked or did not move. Checked against the cockpit model:

PushbuttonBeforeNow
A/THR instinctive disconnect pbs (thrust levers 1 and 4)Not clickable (PUSH_THROTTLE_1/2)Clickable (ATHR_DISCONNECT_ENG1/2): they disconnect the A/THR through the existing FBW interface
EVAC HORN SHUT OFFNot clickable (PUSH_OVHD_EVAC_HORN)Clickable (PUSH_OVHD_EVAC_HORN_OFF)
DFDR EVENT, ACMS TRIGGER (right pedestal)Did not moveMove when pressed (ANIM_PUSH_DFDR_EVENT / ANIM_PUSH_ACMS_TRIGGER)

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
DFDR EVENT and ACMS TRIGGER only move.There is no flight data recorder or ACMS function behind them; their tooltips still say (Inop.).None.

How to use

  1. Press an A/THR instinctive disconnect pushbutton on thrust lever 1 or 4 to disconnect the A/THR.
  2. Press EVAC HORN SHUT OFF on the overhead EVAC panel, DFDR EVENT or ACMS TRIGGER on the right pedestal as on the aircraft.

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1A380X in flight, A/THR active. Click the instinctive disconnect pushbutton on thrust lever 1.The pushbutton moves and the A/THR disconnects (A/THR leaves the FMA).
2Engage the A/THR again and click the pushbutton on thrust lever 4.Same result.
3Click EVAC HORN SHUT OFF on the overhead EVAC panel.The pushbutton can be clicked and moves.
4Click DFDR EVENT and ACMS TRIGGER on the right pedestal.Each one moves in and out when pressed.

2. Clickable RESET panel buttons and side slip probe vanes

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Overhead RESET panels; nose side slip probes  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-03); the user accepted that 42 buttons only drive their variable, and confirmed the vane direction.

What it is for

All 52 circuit breakers of the overhead RESET panels can now be pulled and pushed, with their FCOM label as tooltip. Each one writes L:A32NX_RESET_PANEL_<name> (true = pulled, computer reset).

  • Read by the avionics: ARPT NAV, FMC A, FMC B, FMC C, FWS 1, FWS 2, AESU 1, AESU 2, NSS AVNCS, NSS FLT OPS.
  • The other 42 (AICU, ATC, AVS, BSCS, CIDS, CPCS, DSMS, DTLNK ROUTER, EIPMs, ESS TR, TR 1, TR 2A, FLAPS, FQMS, GCU, LGCIS, PACK CTL, PAX BBAND, SCS, SDF, SLATS, TCS, VCS) are clickable but no system reads them yet.

The three side slip probe vanes on the nose now follow the side slip angle (sim INCIDENCE BETA, up to 60 degrees each side): they line up with the airflow.

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
42 RESET buttons do nothing in the systems.The systems they reset are not wired to the RESET panel yet.Use the 10 wired ones to test a reset.

How to use

  1. Hover a RESET panel button to read its label; click to pull it (reset), click again to push it back.
  2. Look at the nose from the outside view to see the side slip vanes.

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1Hover the buttons of the overhead RESET panels.Each one shows its FCOM label as tooltip.
2Click a button.It pulls out; click again: it pushes in.
3Click one of the 42 not wired buttons, for example a PACK CTL button.It moves; no system reacts.
4In flight, outside view on the nose. Fly a steady sideslip with the rudder (for example right rudder).The three side slip vanes turn to line up with the airflow; centred in coordinated flight.

3. FMC-C reset button checked in the all-FMCs-reset test

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Overhead RESET panels: FMC A, FMC B, FMC C  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-03): FMC-A and FMC-B pulled, the FMS kept the plan with FMC-C in. Unit tests: FmcResetPanel.spec.ts.

What it is for

The FMS stops and resets when all the FMC reset buttons are pulled. The check read FMC-B twice and never FMC-C, so pulling FMC-A and FMC-B alone reset the FMS and lost the flight plan while FMC-C was still in. Per A380 FCOM DSC-22-FMS-30 FMC-C is the standby FMC, and FMS 1 and FMS 2 are only lost when FMC-A, FMC-B and FMC-C all fail (PRO-ABN-ECAM-10-22-FMS AUTO FLT FMS 1+2 FAULT). The check now covers A, B and C.

What you should see

SituationWhat you should see
FMC-A and FMC-B pulled, FMC-C inThe FMS keeps running; the flight plan stays.
FMC-A, FMC-B and FMC-C pulledThe FMS stops and resets (the flight plan is lost), as before.

How to use

  1. Pull FMC A, FMC B and / or FMC C on the overhead RESET panels to reset those FMCs; push them back in to restore.

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1A380X with a flight plan in the MFD F-PLN page.The plan is shown.
2Pull the FMC A and FMC B reset buttons.The flight plan stays on the MFD (FMC-C still in).
3Pull FMC C as well.The FMS resets: the flight plan is lost.

4. AUTOLAND light press-to-test and flashing

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Glareshield, captain and F/O AUTOLAND lights  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-04).

What it is for

A380 FCOM DSC-22-FG-80-100: the AUTOLAND light flashes in autoland below 200 ft RA when the autoland conditions are lost, and "as long as the AUTOLAND light is pressed, the AUTOLAND light flashes" (light test). The cockpit lamp code cannot read the sim time, so the old flashing expression never lit the light. The flashing (1 Hz: half on, half off) is now computed in the systems host, for the light test and for the autoland warning.

What you should see

SituationWhat you should see
AUTOLAND light held pressedIt flashes red at 1 Hz until released.
Autoland below 200 ft RA, autoland conditions lostBoth AUTOLAND lights flash (the warning itself is the existing FBW logic).

How to use

  1. Press and hold the AUTOLAND light on the glareshield to test it (cockpit preparation).

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1A380X powered, on the ground. Press and hold the captain AUTOLAND light.It flashes red, about once per second.
2Release it.It goes off.
3Repeat with the F/O AUTOLAND light.Same.

5. MFP vanes follow the angle of attack

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Nose multifunction probes (outside view)  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK (2026-10-04).

What it is for

The angle of attack vanes of the three multifunction probes on the nose (one on the left, two on the right, A380 FCOM DSC-34-10-30-10) now follow the angle of attack (sim INCIDENCE ALPHA, up to 60 degrees each way, smoothed so that the jumps at very low speed on the ground do not show).

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
The vanes do not move without the companion model change.The MFP_ANGLE1..3 animations are in the A380_FUSELAGE model of the aircraft-large-files repository (fork branch feat/a380x-mfp-vane-animations, b8fb739).Install that model change with this branch.

How to use

  1. Look at the nose probes from the outside view in flight.

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1A380X in flight, outside view on the nose probes. Pitch up slowly to raise the angle of attack.The three MFP vanes turn: their trailing tip rises with the angle of attack.
2Lower the nose to a small angle of attack.The vanes come back near their neutral position.
