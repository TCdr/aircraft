1. Staggered power-up of the displays, RMPs, OITs and ISIS

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: Whole cockpit, at power-up  |  Status: Measured in the sim with PresentMon, not sim-tested by the user. 18 unit tests.

What it is for

When EXT PWR, a generator or the batteries supplied the cockpit, every display unit, RMP, OIT and ISIS left its off state in the same frame. Each then runs its normal self test, so all of them ended it together about 15 s later and laid out their first picture in one frame. In the sim that was a run of slow frames of about 1.4 s at the end of the self tests. Now each unit waits for its own place in a fixed order, 0.25 s after the one before, so the pictures appear one after another and the load is spread.

What you should see

Each unit starts its self test at its slot (counted from the moment its own supply appears); the picture follows about 15 s later, in the same order:

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
The order and the 0.25 s spacing are a design choice.The A380 FCOM gives no power-up timing for these units. The spacing is wide enough for the first picture of one unit to be drawn before the next unit starts.None: only the order of appearance is affected, not the normal self test of each unit.
A stutter of about 1.4 s remains right at the EXT PWR press.It is not caused by the displays (it is the same with the staggered power-up) and is not in the HTML instruments; the cause was not found.None known.

How to use

  1. Nothing to operate: switch the electrical power on as usual (batteries, EXT PWR, generators).
  2. The displays, RMPs, OITs and ISIS start one after another; wait for their normal self tests to end (about 15 s for the displays; the ISIS run their own, longer INIT).

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1A380X cold and dark, on the ground. Press EXT PWR (or switch the batteries on).The power-up begins. The units do not all come up in the same instant.
2Watch the cockpit for about 20 s.The pictures appear one after another in the order of the table: CAPT PFD first, then F/O PFD, CAPT ND, F/O ND, EWD, SD, CAPT MFD, F/O MFD, then the RMP, the OITs and the ISIS. Each ends in its normal picture.
3Switch the display brightness of one unit (for example the F/O MFD) to off, then turn it up again after the cockpit is fully up.The unit comes back at once through its normal self test, with no extra wait.
4Optional, with a frame-time recorder (for example PresentMon) running through steps 1 and 2.No run of slow frames (about 1.4 s) at the end of the display self tests. A stutter right at the EXT PWR press can remain (see the limitations).

2. OIT, SND and printer: no rewriting of unchanged values

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: OIT, lower ISIS (SND), pedestal printer  |  Status: Measured with PresentMon together with the staggered power-up, not sim-tested by the user. 9 unit tests (OIT).

What it is for

The sim's browser renderer redraws a gauge on any write to the page, even when the value written is the same as before. Three instruments wrote unchanged values all the time, which cost work for nothing. They now write only on a change; what you see does not change.

  • OIT: the self-test and content layers are written once per visibility change, not on every brightness, power or failure update. The brightness is rounded to whole percent, so a value that never settles exactly does not count as a change.
  • SND: the visibility style is set only when it changes (before: every frame).
  • Pedestal printer: an empty page now shares one constant, so the "same page as before" check holds and the paper is no longer reset ten times a second.

What you should see
