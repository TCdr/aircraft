1. Taxi route page

Aircraft: A32NX and A380X  |  Where: flyPad > Ground > Taxi  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user at RJTT and Montreal CYUL (the fixes in "Locate the aircraft..."), and on both aircraft: OK. Unit tests of the route finder.

What it is for

Before taxiing, the crew gets a taxi clearance from ATC such as "taxi to holding point runway 09 via M, K, A". On an unknown airport, following it on the chart is where mistakes happen: a wrong turn, a runway crossed without clearance. This page draws the route of the clearance on the airport map, lists the runways it crosses and stops it at the runway holding point.

The real A380 has no taxi routing: its OANS (the airport map on the ND) only lets the crew place green flags and white crosses (FCOM DSC-34-10-70-20), and MSFS has no datalink taxi clearance (D-TAXI). So the route is built on the flyPad, and the cockpit displays stay as the FCOM describes them. On the A380, the accepted route is marked on the OANS with those green flags (see "A380X: the accepted taxi route on the OANS" below).

  • Departure (default): from a gate, a ramp or cargo stand, or the aircraft position, to a runway entry.
  • Arrival: from the runway exit you plan to take (or the aircraft position) to your gate.
  • The page suggests the shortest route; you type the taxiways of the ATC clearance and accept it.

Before you start

  • Navigraph account linked in the flyPad settings (third party options). The airport map comes from the Navigraph airport database, the same data as the A380 OANS. Without it the page says "No airport database".
  • Airport: type the ICAO code, or press ORIG / DEST to take the airports of the SimBrief flight plan loaded in the flyPad. The line below shows how many gates, ramp and cargo stands the map has.
  • An airport that is not in the Navigraph airport database shows "Not in the Navigraph airport database".

Departure: from the gate to the runway

  1. Keep Departure selected and enter the airport.
  2. In FROM, keep Gate/ramp/cargo and type your gate. While you type, the gates that match appear as buttons under the field: tap one. You can also tap the gate on the map (zoom in to see the names).
  3. In TO, choose the runway. The entry is set to the full length entry; choose another one for an intersection departure.
  4. The shortest route appears on the map as a dashed magenta line ending at the HOLD bar of the runway holding point.

Gate names are found even when they are written differently on the map: "16" finds "016", spaces and dashes do not matter. If nothing matches, the airport map uses another name (for example a letter prefix): tap the gate on the map instead.

Runway entries

The Entry list shows every taxiway that enters the runway, in order from the threshold. The first one is the full length entry; the others show the runway length remaining from them, to check an intersection departure against your takeoff performance. On the map, the entries of the selected runway are the cyan dots with their names.

The ATC clearance

Type the taxiways of your clearance in the order ATC gives them, separated by spaces (commas and dashes work too). The route then follows them strictly, in that order:

  • Short unnamed connecting lines between two cleared taxiways are allowed.
  • The runway entry can be left out: "via M, K, A" to a holding point still ends on the entry you chose.
  • While the typed route is not accepted, the box reads "Route along the clearance (not accepted)" and the line stays dashed.

SUGGEST copies the taxiways of the suggested route into the clearance field: a quick start when ATC clears you along the obvious way. ACCEPT confirms the route: the line turns solid. Any change (gate, runway, entry, clearance) takes the acceptance away.

Runway crossings

Every runway the route crosses is circled in red on the map with its name, and listed in red under the route ("Crossing runway 01/19"). A runway crossing always needs its own ATC clearance: stop at the holding point before it and ask if you do not have it.

Arrival: from the runway exit to the gate

  1. Select Arrival and enter the airport (DEST fills it from the flight plan).
  2. In FROM, keep Runway exit, choose the landing runway and the exit you plan to take. The exits are those of the landing calculator, with their distance from the threshold.
  3. In TO, type or tap the gate.
  4. Check the route, type the clearance if ATC gives another one, and ACCEPT.
TipPrepare the arrival route during the descent briefing with the exit from the landing calculator: after landing, the page is ready. If you vacate by another exit, choose it (or use the aircraft position) and the route updates.

From the aircraft position

Choose Aircraft in FROM to start the route where the aircraft is, for example after a pushback, or when ATC changes the route while you taxi. The page takes the position when you select it; press Update to take it again. The route starts on the taxi line nearest to the aircraft and prefers its heading: it does not start by turning back if there is a way ahead.

"The aircraft is not at this airport" means the aircraft is more than 8 km from the airport of the page.

Locate the aircraft, taxiway names of several words, proposed entry

  • Locate button (pin, under the zoom buttons): centres the map on the aircraft and zooms in until the stand names show. It is greyed when the aircraft is not at this airport.
  • Taxiways named with several words (e.g. SOUTH RAMP, NORTH RAMP at Montreal CYUL) are read as one taxiway in the clearance: type them as the map shows them, or press SUGGEST.
  • The runway entry proposed first is the first one from the threshold you can reach without crossing a runway. At CYUL, runway 24L full length (R) is across 06R/24L from the terminal: the entry proposed is the first one on the terminal side; choose R in the Entry list if ATC clears you across.

The map

  • Drag the map to move it; + / - zoom; the frame button fits the route (or the whole airport).
  • The map is north up. Aprons are lighter grey, taxiways dark grey, runways black with a white edge. The thin yellow lines are the taxi guidance lines the route follows.
  • Taxiway names are shown in yellow; the taxiways of the route in magenta. Gate names appear when you zoom in.
  • Tap a gate on the map to choose it (when the page asks for a gate).

2. A380X: the accepted taxi route on the OANS

Aircraft: A380X  |  Where: ND (OANS) after ACCEPT on the flyPad Taxi page  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK.

What it is for

The A380 FCOM has no taxi route display: the OANS lets the crew mark points of the airport with green flags (DSC-34-10-70-20). When the crew accepts a route on the flyPad, the A380X marks it with those flags, so the route can be followed on the ND.

On the A380: the route on the OANS

When you accept the route on the A380 flyPad, green flags appear on the OANS (the airport map on the ND) at the start of each taxiway of the route and at the gate or holding point. They are the crew flags of the FCOM ("to locate or mark a given point on the airport"), so you can remove them like any flag: click one and DELETE, or ERASE ALL SYMBOLS in the ND menu. Flags you placed yourself are never touched.

  • The flags appear when the OANS displays that airport (they come back if the OANS reloads it).
  • A new accepted route replaces the flags of the previous one; a route that is no longer accepted removes them.
  • The A320 has no OANS: nothing else changes on it.
NoteTested in the sim by the user: OK: the flags are set on ACCEPT and removed when the route changes.

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do

An Aircraft start is lost when leaving the Taxi page, so its flags go away when you come back.The aircraft start is local to the page.Select Aircraft again and ACCEPT.

How to use

  1. Accept a route on the flyPad Taxi page (A380X).
  2. On the ND, select the OANS (ARC or ROSE, a zoom range) at that airport: the flags show the start of each taxiway and the end of the route.

3. Airport ATC frequencies on the taxi page

Aircraft: A32NX and A380X  |  Where: flyPad > Ground > Taxi, bottom left of the map  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user: OK.

What it is for

Before taxi the crew needs the ground, tower and delivery frequencies of the airport. The taxi page shows them in a box laid out like the frequency box of an airport chart, for the origin, the destination or the airport of the page. The Navigraph airport map has no frequencies: they come from the sim's own airport data.

What you see

  • A green Frequencies button at the bottom left of the map; it opens the FREQUENCIES box (X closes it).
  • Airport buttons at the top: ORIG and DEST of the SimBrief flight plan loaded in the flyPad, and Airport when the page airport is another one.
  • One row per chart label, in the chart order: ATIS, AWOS, ASOS, CTL, APP, DEP, TWR, GND, DLV, CTAF, UNICOM, MULTICOM, FSS; the frequencies in MHz, two columns, without duplicates, in the order of the sim data. Other types (ILS...) are left out.
  • Messages: "Enter an airport", "Loading the frequencies...", "Not in the sim's airport data", "No frequency for this airport in the sim's airport data".

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
The list is the sim's, not the chart's.The chart's selection of frequencies is in no data the aircraft can read; MSFS lists all its frequencies.Check the chart for the one to use.
No station names, no tuning.The box is a reference, like the chart box.Tune the RMP as usual.

How to use

  1. On the Taxi page, press Frequencies (bottom left of the map).
  2. Choose ORIG, DEST or the page airport; read the frequency you need.

4. Services page linked to GSX

Aircraft: A32NX and A380X  |  Where: flyPad > Ground > Services, GSX panel  |  Status: First version tested in the sim by the user with GSX 4 (A380X, RJTT): four bugs found. Their fixes on this branch (panel position, counters, greyed switch, status lines) are not sim-tested on this classic page. The Material 3 Services page of core/efb-redesign, which carries them with further GSX fixes, was tested in the sim (A32NX: 170 passengers given to GSX, count 0 to 170, Done).

What it is for

With GSX installed, the ground services are GSX's, but the flyPad Services buttons called the sim's own vehicles. A GSX services switch now links the page to GSX through the GSX Remote API (version 2, local server on port 8744): the service buttons request the GSX services and show what GSX is doing. With the switch off the page works as before.

What you see

  • The GSX panel under the right-hand group of buttons (GPU, cargo door): the switch and a status line: "GSX not found: start GSX, with its Remote control server on (GSX settings)" (switch greyed), "GSX is starting...", "GSX found" (switch off) or "Service buttons linked to GSX, GSX menus and messages shown here".
  • Linked: Jet Bridge, Stairs, Fuel Truck, GPU and Catering Truck request the GSX services. The Baggage Truck button is hidden: GSX loads the bags during boarding.
  • The panel adds the services without an aircraft button: Deboarding, Boarding, Pushback (Departure), De-ice, Water, Lavatory, Cleaning, named as GSX names them.
  • Under each linked button a status line: On its way, Waiting, GSX's own phase (or In progress), Leaving, Done, with its count; a progress bar for the fuel truck from the "x / y kg" refuelling status. Button colours follow the service: amber when called or waiting, green while performed, amber and locked while leaving, dim when GSX cannot serve it.
  • Linking switches on the flyPad GSX Fueling and GSX Payload Synchronization (Settings > 3rd Party Options), so GSX boards the passengers and loads the fuel set on the Payload and Fuel pages.
  • While linked, GSX is under remote control: no GSX pop-up menu or notification in the sim. GSX's questions (menus that stay open, e.g. the choice of operator) and its messages appear on the panel; tap an answer, or "Close the GSX menu".
  • A request GSX refuses (e.g. boarding before the doors are open) gives the message "GSX did not accept <service>" with GSX's reason.

Needs GSX with its Remote control server on.The link uses the GSX Remote API (GSX 4).Turn on the Remote control server in the GSX settings.

  2. flyPad > Ground > Services: when the panel says "GSX found", turn on GSX services.
  3. Request the services with the buttons and the GSX panel; answer GSX's questions on the panel.
  4. Turn the switch off to go back to the FBW services.

5. GSX external power sync when it is enabled

Aircraft: A32NX and A380X  |  Where: flyPad > Settings > 3rd Party Options > GSX External Power Synchronization  |  Status: Tested in the sim by the user (A380X, GSX 4, RJTT): EXT AVAIL on the ELEC panel with the GSX GPU, OK.

What it is for

The FBW option GSX External Power Synchronization makes the external power available (L:A32NX_EXT_PWR_AVAIL) when the GSX GPU is connected or the jetway is requested or connected, until pushback. It only looked at GSX when a GSX state changed: enabling the option with the GPU or the jetway already connected did nothing until the next GSX change. It now checks GSX at once when it is enabled.

6. Taxi route along real ATC clearances

Aircraft: A32NX and A380X  |  Where: flyPad > Ground > Taxi, clearance field  |  Status: Not sim-tested (added after the user's RJTT report of the same day; unit tests in taxiRoute.spec.ts "Taxi route along an ATC clearance", on a synthetic RJTT-like map).

What it is for

At RJTT the clearance W A E C1 C1 typed from the aircraft after pushback gave "No route along these taxiways", and a full BeyondATC instruction ("via L4 cross runway 34L, taxi to holding point C1, runway 34R, via L4, A4B, W4, W, A, E, C1") could not be used. Real clearances leave out the apron taxiways, repeat the holding point, name runway exits and come as sentences. The route finder now accepts them:

  • Typed list or pasted instruction: the clearance field reads a list of taxiways or a pasted ATC instruction. Phraseology words (VIA, TAXI, TO, HOLDING POINT...), runway designators, the stand after STAND or GATE and frequencies are left out. The runways after CROSS (RUNWAY) are the cleared crossings. The clearance limit (after TO or HOLDING POINT) is the last taxiway. HOLD SHORT (OF) a taxiway is left out. "A-4B" is read as the airport name A4B. A name said twice in a row is one taxiway.
  • Apron taxiways before the clearance: before the first cleared taxiway, taxiways that are not in the clearance may lead to it, within 2000 m of the start. They cost more (x1.5), so the route joins the clearance as soon as it can. A cleared taxiway is never used out of order.
  • Uncleared runway crossings: in that first part, a runway crossing that the clearance does not mention costs a large penalty: the route goes round when it can; the crossing still shows on the map. "cross runway 34L" in the clearance removes the penalty.
  • Runway exit lines named in the clearance are cleared taxiways; a clearance that starts with the landing exit ("B1 A K M") works. Exit lines are listed in the route taxiways (SUGGEST), except the start exit.
  • Unknown names: the page lists the cleared names that are not a taxiway of the airport map, under the clearance field in amber ("Not a taxiway of the airport map: X9"). The field placeholder now says "e.g. A K M, or paste the ATC instruction".

  3. Check the route, any "Crossing runway" line and any "Not a taxiway of the airport map" line, then ACCEPT.

7. GSX power sync: the setting is re-read, so enabling it takes effect

Aircraft: A32NX and A380X  |  Where: flyPad > Settings > 3rd Party Options > GSX External Power Synchronization  |  Status: Not sim-tested (added; unit tests: GsxSync.spec.ts, 3, all failing without the change).

What it is for

Follow-up of the feature above. The flyPad switch stores the setting and signals the change through a listener that the extras host (the code that runs the GSX sync) never registers, so the change did not always reach it: turning the sync on with the GSX GPU or jetway already connected could still leave EXT PWR unavailable. The extras host now re-reads the stored setting (at most once a second) and checks the GSX power source again when the setting goes from off to on.

Limitations and why

LimitationWhyWhat to do
Up to one second between the switch and EXT AVAIL.The setting is polled once a second.-

How to use

  1. As above: turn on GSX External Power Synchronization, before or after GSX connects its GPU or jetway.

How to test

#Do thisExpected result
1Cold and dark at a GSX gate, sync OFF, GSX GPU connected.No EXT AVAIL from the sync.
2Turn the flyPad GSX power sync on.EXT PWR AVAIL within a second.

What PR testers should check

  • Turn the flyPad GSX power sync on with the GSX GPU connected: EXT PWR AVAIL at once.
  • Not sim-tested yet.

8. External power follows the sim's ground power (GSX GPU, sim GPU, jetway)

Aircraft: A32NX and A380X  |  Where: Overhead ELEC panel EXT PWR; flyPad > Ground > Services GPU button  |  Status: Not sim-tested (added; unit tests: GPUManagement.spec.ts, 10, failing first).

What it is for

The aircraft read the sim's external power only on the first frame and when the flyPad GPU button was used. A GPU or a jetway connected after the flight loaded (GSX 4, the sim GPU) did nothing until the GPU door moved. Now the external power follows the sim's ground power, whoever connects it.
